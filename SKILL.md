# noAI

When creating or revising a website, avoid the visual and writing conventions commonly associated with generic AI-generated design. Aim for a deliberate, distinctive result that fits the site's audience, subject, and brand. Use specific design choices that feel considered rather than defaulting to familiar AI landing-page patterns.

Avoid purple-ish gradients, neon colors, bland blue palettes, and Inter-like fonts. Avoid placing status pills above the main header. Do not use emojis or full caps text for display copy. Choose typography, color, spacing, and layout that suit the actual product and give the site a recognizable character.

Avoid generic promotional phrases such as “Unlock your potential,” “Elevate your experience,” “Seamlessly integrate,” “Supercharge your workflow,” “Powered by innovation,” “The future of...,” “All-in-one solution,” and “Experience the power of AI.” Treat these as examples of a broader pattern: replace vague hype with plain, concrete language that explains what the product does, who it helps, and what the visitor can do next. Avoid close variations and other empty slogans.

Apply these preferences with judgment. Preserve explicit brand requirements and user-provided copy when asked, and keep the interface accessible, legible, and appropriate to its context. These rules guide visual and copy decisions; they do not require removing accurate references to AI when those references are relevant to the site's purpose.

If the user explicitly asks for one of these styles or phrases, follow their direction for that request. When the user asks to expand or customize these guidelines, add the requested preferences and examples to the skill.

Also avoid predictable AI landing-page compositions such as a centered hero with an oversized vague headline, a glowing orb or abstract gradient blob, floating glass cards, excessive rounded rectangles, and a row of identical feature cards with simple line icons. Avoid decorative sparkle motifs, synthetic 3D shapes, stock-looking robot or brain imagery, and gratuitous AI-generated illustrations that do not explain the product. Do not add fake dashboards, invented metrics, fabricated testimonials, or logos presented as customer proof.

Avoid generic interface filler such as “What if…?” hooks, repetitive benefit headings, vague feature labels, placeholder chat conversations, and fake prompts that do not reflect real user tasks. Avoid presenting every product as an AI assistant or wrapping ordinary features in an AI label. Use real product screenshots, concrete examples, meaningful details, and varied layouts when they help visitors understand the offering. Treat these as signals to question, not a rigid checklist: retain a pattern when it serves the brand, content, or usability.
